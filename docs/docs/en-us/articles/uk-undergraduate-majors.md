# An In-Depth Look at Five UK Undergraduate Programmes

## Imperial, UCL, UAL: Mathematics, Media, and Film Production Compared

These five programmes come from three UK universities:

- Imperial College London: Mathematics BSc
- UCL (University College London): Mathematics BSc, Media BA
- LCC (London College of Communication), part of UAL (University of the Arts London): Film and Television BA, Film Practice BA

My analysis is based on the 2027 undergraduate entry information published on the official websites. The goal is not only to explain what each programme teaches, but also to keep one important premise in mind:

These five programmes are the application choices of a single student.

That means we need to look at the connections between the programmes, whether the application materials are compatible, and what academic direction this combination of choices reflects.

## 1. Overall Comparison of the Five Programmes

| University and programme | Core focus | Degree | Duration | Main training goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Imperial Mathematics | Pure mathematics, applied mathematics, mathematical modeling | BSc | 3 years | Mathematical theory and quantitative research |
| UCL Mathematics | Pure mathematics, applied mathematics, mathematical methods | BSc | 3 years | Mathematical research, modeling, analysis |
| UCL Media | Digital media, moving image, games, interactive media | BA | 3 years | Digital media creation and critical study |
| UAL Film and Television | Film and television content production | BA (Hons) | 3 years | Screen production and industry practice |
| UAL Film Practice | Filmmaking, storytelling, and creative practice | BA (Hons) | 3 years | Filmmakers and professional production crew |

Where:

- BSc (Bachelor of Science): a science bachelor's degree.
- BA (Bachelor of Arts): an arts/humanities bachelor's degree.
- BA (Hons): a Bachelor of Arts with Honours, the common form of undergraduate honours degree in the UK. It does not mean the two UAL programmes rank one level above UCL's BA.

The five programmes can be roughly grouped into three directions:

- **Direction A: Mathematics and quantitative science**: Imperial Mathematics + UCL Mathematics
- **Direction B: Interdisciplinary digital media**: UCL Media
- **Direction C: Professional film and television production**: UAL Film and Television + UAL Film Practice

These are not five closely related programmes. The gap between the two mathematics programmes and the two film production programmes is wide, and UCL Media is the more interdisciplinary option sitting in between.

## 2. Imperial College London — Mathematics BSc

- University: Imperial College London
- Programme: Mathematics BSc
- Location: South Kensington, London
- Duration: 3 years
- UCAS code: G100
- Official course page: [Imperial Mathematics BSc](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/study/courses/undergraduate/mathematics-bsc/)

### 1. What does this programme actually teach?

Imperial's Mathematics is an undergraduate programme that gives equal weight to mathematical theory and its applications, with very high academic demands.

It does not continue teaching problem-solving techniques the way high school math does. Instead, it asks students to understand:

- Why does a mathematical concept hold?
- What conditions does a theorem need to satisfy?
- How do you construct a rigorous mathematical proof?
- How can mathematics describe real-world problems?
- How can abstract mathematical theory be applied to computation, statistics, and science?

For example, high school math might ask a student to find the limit of a function, while university mathematics goes further and asks:

How exactly is a limit rigorously defined? Under what conditions does a limit exist? Can a given result be proved?

As a result, Imperial's mathematics programme places more emphasis on abstract thinking, the ability to write proofs, and an understanding of mathematical structures.

### 2. Main content over the three years

| Year | Example modules | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Year 1 | Analysis 1 | Mathematical analysis: limits, sequences, series, and functions |
| Year 1 | Linear Algebra and Groups | Linear algebra, vector spaces, linear transformations, and the basics of group theory |
| Year 1 | Calculus and Applications | Calculus and its applications |
| Year 1 | Probability and Statistics | Probability theory, statistics, and data analysis |
| Year 1 | Introduction to Computation | Python programming and mathematical computation |
| Year 2 | Analysis 2 | Higher-dimensional analysis and topological concepts |
| Year 2 | Linear Algebra and Numerical Analysis | Further linear algebra and numerical methods |
| Year 2 | Multi-variable Calculus and Differential Equations | Multivariable calculus and differential equations |
| Year 3 | Mathematical Finance | Mathematical finance and option pricing |
| Year 3 | Methods for Data Science | Mathematical methods for data science |
| Year 3 | Markov Processes | Markov processes and stochastic models |
| Year 3 | Mathematical Logic / Number Theory | Pure mathematics areas such as mathematical logic and number theory |

These are representative modules listed on the official website; not every student has to take every module in the table. Imperial offers more than 50 specialist modules for students to choose from within the programme rules.

### 3. Academic characteristics

**First, mathematical proof is central.**

Students need to gradually shift from computational thinking to theoretical thinking. Excellent high school math grades do not necessarily mean a student will adapt immediately to university proof-based courses.

**Second, there is a wide range of choice.**

After the foundation modules, students can develop in different directions:

- Pure mathematics: algebra, analysis, number theory, geometry
- Applied mathematics: fluid dynamics, differential equations, scientific modeling
- Probability and statistics: stochastic processes, statistical inference
- Computational mathematics: numerical analysis, data science
- Mathematical finance: risk, pricing, and financial models

**Third, exams and independent study carry a lot of weight.**

The typical assessment structure given on the official website is mainly written exams, alongside research projects, coursework, and presentations.

### 4. 2027 entry requirements

| Item | Official requirement |
| --- | --- |
| A-Level | A\*A\*A to A\*A\*AA |
| Key subjects | Mathematics A\*, Further Mathematics A\* |
| IB minimum | 39 points |
| IB mathematics requirement | HL Mathematics 7 |
| Mathematics admissions test | TMUA |
| Annual tuition fee for international students | £44,850 (2027 entry) |

For IB mathematics, Imperial prefers Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (AA), but also accepts HL Applications and Interpretation (AI). For A-Level students whose school does not offer Further Mathematics, Imperial has a mechanism for considering special circumstances, so it should not simply be read as meaning that anyone without Further Mathematics has no chance at all.

### 5. Career paths after graduation

Imperial mathematics graduates can go on to consider:

- Research in mathematics, statistics, or applied mathematics
- Quantitative finance and risk modeling
- Data science, machine learning, and artificial intelligence
- Software algorithms and mathematical modeling
- Actuarial work, consulting, and business analytics

Keep in mind that a mathematics degree provides a theoretical and analytical foundation; it does not mean a graduate automatically has the full professional skill set of an AI engineer or a quantitative researcher.

**Good fit for:** Students who genuinely enjoy abstract mathematics, logical reasoning, and solving hard problems, and who want to keep studying advanced mathematics, rather than choosing it just because they have good math grades or because the university is famous.

## 3. UCL — Mathematics BSc

- University: University College London
- Programme: Mathematics BSc
- Location: Bloomsbury, London
- Duration: 3 years
- UCAS code: G100
- Official course page: [UCL Mathematics BSc](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/study/prospective-students/undergraduate/courses/mathematics-bsc)

### 1. Programme positioning

UCL Mathematics is likewise an undergraduate programme centered on mathematics as a discipline in its own right.

It covers four key foundational areas:

1. Algebra: mathematical structures, matrices, groups, vector spaces, and more.
2. Analysis: limits, continuity, differentiation, integration, and more advanced analytical theory.
3. Applied Mathematics: using mathematics to study motion, physical systems, and real-world models.
4. Mathematical Methods: differential equations, numerical methods, computational techniques, and more.

The course emphasizes choosing advanced modules on top of a solid foundation, and offers more than 30 specialist optional modules.

### 2. How the three years are structured

**Year 1: Building a mathematical foundation**

Includes analysis, algebra, probability and statistics, dynamics, further calculus, programming, and vector calculus.

For example, Programming and Vector Calculus combines programming with mathematical tools, but that does not mean students are taking a computer science degree.

**Year 2: Gradual specialization**

Students continue with:

- Complex Analysis
- Further Linear Algebra
- Ordinary Differential Equations
- Fourier Methods and Partial Differential Equations

and choose modules from areas such as number theory, groups and rings, geometry, and computational mathematics.

**Year 3: Going deeper into specialist areas**

Students can go on to take options such as:

- Measure Theory
- Functional Analysis
- Galois Theory
- Algebraic Topology
- Geophysical Fluid Dynamics

These subjects are already well into advanced mathematical theory, rather than a simple extension of high school math.

### 3. How does UCL Mathematics differ from Imperial's?

| Dimension | Imperial Mathematics | UCL Mathematics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disciplinary foundation | Pure and applied mathematics | Pure and applied mathematics |
| Year 1 focus | Proof, analysis, algebra, computation, research | Analysis, algebra, probability, applied mathematics, programming |
| Upper-year choice | More than 50 specialist modules | More than 30 specialist modules |
| Interdisciplinary room | Course mechanisms such as I-Explore | Modules from other departments such as economics, statistics, computer science, and languages, subject to conditions |
| Assessment | Mainly written exams, combined with coursework and research | Mainly written exams, with a small amount of coursework |
| Additional tests in the application | TMUA | STEP is not required on the standard A-Level route when the specified conditions are met; alternative routes may require STEP or AEA |

Both are rigorous mathematics degrees. You cannot conclude from the university names alone that one leans toward research and the other toward applications; the actual direction depends largely on the modules a student chooses in the last two years.

### 4. 2027 entry requirements

| Item | Official requirement |
| --- | --- |
| A-Level standard requirement | A\*A\*A |
| Key subjects | A\* in both Mathematics and Further Mathematics |
| A-Level alternative route | A\*AA, with A\* and A in Mathematics and Further Mathematics, combined with STEP 2 or a Mathematics AEA Distinction |
| IB standard requirement | 40 points, 20 points across 3 HL subjects, HL Mathematics 7 |
| IB alternative route | 39 points, meeting the HL conditions, combined with STEP 2 or an AEA Distinction |
| IB mathematics course | Only HL Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches is accepted |
| Annual tuition fee for international students | £44,400 (2027/28) |

These are the standard entry requirements; special policies such as contextual offers are not covered here.

### 5. Career paths after graduation

UCL mathematics graduates can likewise choose:

Mathematical research, finance, actuarial work, statistics, data science, tech companies, consulting, education, and more.

**Good fit for:** Students with a strong mathematical foundation who want to study rigorous mathematical theory while keeping open the option of moving into finance, data science, computer science, or other applications of mathematics.

## 4. UCL — Media BA

- University: University College London
- Programme: Media BA
- Location: UCL East, Stratford, London
- Duration: 3 years
- UCAS code: P300
- Official course page: [UCL Media BA](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/study/prospective-students/undergraduate/courses/media-ba)

### 1. What kind of media programme is this?

Of the five programmes, this is the one that most deserves its own explanation, because it is easy to misunderstand.

UCL Media BA is neither a traditional journalism and communication programme nor a film school course that simply teaches cinematography, editing, and directing.

What it emphasizes is:

> Understanding how modern digital media shape society, and engaging in creative practice through film, games, interactive technology, and emerging media.

The course combines three layers:

- Media Theory: how media affect culture, society, identity, the spread of information, and people's behavior.
- Creative Media Practice: making short films, digital content, games, and interactive work.
- Emerging Media Technologies: exploring technologies such as VR, AR, artificial intelligence, and digital worldbuilding.

This gives it artistic content as well as social science and technology content.

For example:

Suppose a student wants to study how artificial intelligence is changing filmmaking.

In this programme, the student might not only learn how to use AI tools to produce a piece of moving image, but also study the authorship, creative ownership, and cultural impact involved in AI-generated content, and how audiences make sense of such work.

This is the key difference between UCL Media and a traditional film production programme.

### 2. Core course content

| Module | Overview | What it studies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction to Creative Media Practice | Foundations of creative practice | Ideation, practice methods, and production basics |
| Digital Cultures and Society | Digital culture and its social context | Social media, the internet, and social culture |
| Short Fiction Films | Narrative short film | Creating fiction on screen |
| Creative Media Lab | Hands-on creative lab | Creative experiments and practical projects |
| Worldbuilding for Games, Interactive and Cinematic Experiences | Building story worlds across media | Virtual environments, narrative worlds, and interactive experiences |
| Designing for VR/AR | Virtual and augmented reality design | Immersive media experiences |
| Machine Learning and Creative AI | AI for creative work | Applications of artificial intelligence in creative media |
| Game Development Fundamentals | Introduction to game development | Game design and production methods |
| Filmic Fictions | Cinematic storytelling | Building fictional stories through moving image |

Some of these are later optional modules rather than all being compulsory.

### 3. The three-year learning path

**Year 1: Broad exposure to the media field**

Students learn basic filmmaking, digital culture, media theory, games, and interaction design.

The aim at this stage is for students to build broad media literacy, rather than narrowing too early into a single role such as directing, editing, or cinematography.

**Year 2: Choosing a direction of interest**

Students can develop in different directions:

- Film: documentary, fiction, visual storytelling
- Games: game development, interactive experiences
- Technology-driven media: VR/AR, creative AI
- Research: digital culture, media theory, the creative economy

There may also be opportunities to take industry-related practice or work placement modules, depending on places and arrangements.

**Year 3: Completing independent research and creative projects**

Students need to combine research with creative production and demonstrate their abilities through a major project or a dissertation.

Assessment may include not only essays and reports, but also moving-image work, blogs, podcasts, oral presentations, and portfolios.

### 4. 2027 entry requirements

| Item | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| A-Level | AAB |
| Preferred subjects | Arts, humanities, or Computer Science |
| IB | 36 points, 17 points across 3 HL subjects, with no HL subject below 5 |
| Portfolio | Required |
| Annual tuition fee for international students | £44,400 |
| Teaching location | UCL East, not the main Bloomsbury campus |

One point here is easy to overlook:

UCL Media BA requires a portfolio, not just A-Level or IB grades and a personal statement.

The official requirement is to submit the portfolio as a single PDF:

- Up to 6 pieces of work
- A PDF of no more than 12 pages and 50 MB
- May include film, animation, photography, scripts, game prototypes, 3D work, and more
- Must explain the concept, production process, and personal contribution for each piece
- Video and other media can be presented via links in the PDF

The official website also asks for descriptions of the work with accompanying images; when preparing the portfolio, follow the specific submission guidance issued by UCL closely.

### 5. Career paths after graduation

Compared with roles purely in film production, this programme covers a broader range of careers:

- Digital content creation and planning
- Interactive media and immersive experience design
- Games and digital storytelling
- Creative technology and new media projects
- Digital culture research
- Film and video creation and production
- Cultural institutions, arts organizations, and the media industry

**Good fit for:** Students with cross-disciplinary interests in film, digital culture, games, artificial intelligence, and interactive technology, who are willing to do theoretical research and also want to make creative work.

It is worth stressing that although the course touches on machine learning and game development, it is not equivalent to a computer science or AI engineering degree; the depth of technical training and the training goals are different.

## 5. UAL — BA (Hons) Film and Television

- University: University of the Arts London
- College: London College of Communication (LCC)
- Programme: Film and Television BA (Hons)
- In brief: Film and television production (honours degree)
- Duration: 3 years
- UCAS code: W601
- Official course page: [UAL Film and Television](https://www.arts.ac.uk/subjects/animation-interactive-film-and-sound/undergraduate/ba-hons-film-and-television-lcc)

### 1. Core positioning of the programme

This is a clearly defined film and television production programme, with teaching closely tied to the real workflows of the screen industry.

It aims to train people who can take part in producing films, TV dramas, documentaries, TV programmes, and other audiovisual content.

What students learn is not just creative art-making, but also:

- Pre-production: planning and preparation
- Production: shooting and production
- Post-production: editing and finishing
- Screenwriting: writing for the screen
- Studio Production: studio-based production
- Documentary: documentary filmmaking
- Content Development: programme and content development
- Distribution: distribution and circulation

Compared with UCL Media, this programme focuses much more on how screen content actually gets produced.

### 2. Course structure over three years

**Year 1: Learning the foundations of film and television production**

Mainly covers visual language, narrative structure, sound, factual content, and teamwork.

Includes:

- Introduction to Film and Television
- Screen Cultures: Narrative
- Sound, Image, Story
- Screen Cultures: Factual Stories
- Factual

Factual deserves particular attention: it includes training in making documentaries, footage of real events, and other factual television content.

**Year 2: Learning specialized film and television production**

Key modules include:

| Module | Learning focus |
| --- | --- |
| TV Studio Production | Multi-camera shooting, studio production, live and recorded broadcasts |
| The Script | Screenwriting, story structure, and script development |
| Drama | Shooting fiction, working with actors, production workflow |
| Story Development | Story planning, project proposals, creative pitching |
| Screen Performances and Genres | Screen genres, performance, and storytelling techniques |

TV Studio Production in particular shows what is distinctive about this programme.

Students learn how to produce in a multi-camera environment, which is clearly different from the workflow for making a single-camera fiction short.

**Year 3: Major work and career development**

Mainly includes:

- Dissertation (40 credits): final-year research
- Major Project: Portfolio (60 credits): a major creative portfolio project
- Professional Perspectives (20 credits): the screen industry and career development

Students need to demonstrate full production ability and understand how content is commissioned, produced, marketed, and distributed in the industry.

### 3. 2027 entry requirements

| Item | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| UCAS Tariff | 104 points |
| A-Level | Accepts eligible combinations of Level 3 qualifications, with a preference for relevant humanities, arts, and social science subjects |
| IB indicative minimum | 24 points |
| Portfolio | Usually required |
| Interview | May be invited to an online interview |
| Annual tuition fee for international students | £31,510 |
| English | IELTS 6.0, with no component below 5.5 |

104 UCAS points is a qualification points system and should not be read as a fixed, UK-wide A-Level offer grade.

### 4. Portfolio requirements

This is a very important part of the application.

The digital portfolio guidance published by the university includes:

- Up to 10 pages
- At least one video or moving-image piece of no more than 3 minutes
- Optionally, up to two additional moving-image pieces of no more than 60 seconds each
- Showing scripts, storyboards, set design, concept development, shooting plans, and similar material
- Explaining your specific role in pre-production, production, and post-production

The university may also invite applicants to a 15–20 minute online interview.

### 5. Career paths after graduation

Career directions are relatively clear-cut, including:

Film and TV producing, cinematography, editing, screenwriting, documentary production, TV studio production, advertising and commercial video production, sound production, and more.

**Good fit for:** Students who enjoy creating screen content, want to understand both film and television production environments, are willing to work in teams, and want to build experience through real film and TV work.

## 6. UAL — BA (Hons) Film Practice

- University: University of the Arts London — London College of Communication
- Programme: Film Practice BA (Hons)
- In brief: Filmmaking practice (honours degree)
- Duration: 3 years
- UCAS code: W603
- Official course page: [UAL Film Practice](https://www.arts.ac.uk/subjects/animation-interactive-film-and-sound/undergraduate/ba-hons-film-practice-lcc)

### 1. How does this programme differ from Film and Television?

Although both are film-related programmes at LCC, Film Practice concentrates more on filmmaking itself and on collaboration between the specialist roles in film production.

The university emphasizes learning the craft of filmmaking through practice, letting students gradually develop their own filmmaking abilities and professional direction.

It places particular weight on:

- Cinematic storytelling
- Film language and aesthetics
- Making fiction and documentary shorts
- The division of labor and collaboration between production roles
- The full process of a film, from idea to finished work
- Creative philosophy, film history, and the study of films

Note that although Film Practice emphasizes filmmaking, it is not a purely individual directing programme. The university explicitly stresses teamwork, rather than training only a director-centered model of creation.

### 2. What exactly is studied over three years?

**Year 1: Building filmmaking ability**

| Module | Main content |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction to Film Practice | Filmmaking basics and short film planning |
| Documentary | Team production of a creative documentary short |
| Film Style | Film style, aesthetics, and film language |
| Fiction | Making fiction shorts and training in different production roles |

At this stage, students have the chance to learn cinematography, sound, production management, post-production, and other roles, while getting to know the different roles in filmmaking.

**Year 2: Raising the complexity of film production**

Core modules include:

- Studio: filmmaking in a studio
- Story: developing film stories
- Creative Industries: industry practice and career development
- Film Methods: filmmaking methods
- Year 2 Film: completing a film as a team

Year 2 also offers learning opportunities tied to real industry experience, including options such as live client projects or work placements.

**Year 3: Completing a graduation film**

Includes:

- Research Project (40 credits)
- Graduation Film (60 credits)
- Creative Careers (20 credits)

The Graduation Film is the programme's most important final project.

Students need to complete a film in a more complex creative environment and demonstrate their professional abilities.

### 3. 2027 entry requirements

| Item | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| UCAS Tariff | 96 points |
| A-Level | Accepts eligible combinations of Level 3 qualifications, with a preference for arts, humanities, and related subjects |
| IB indicative minimum | 24 points |
| Portfolio | Usually required |
| Interview | May be invited to an online interview |
| Annual tuition fee for international students | £31,510 |
| English | IELTS 6.0, with no component below 5.5 |

### 4. How is the Film Practice portfolio different?

This programme asks for a more targeted portfolio structure.

According to the official website:

**Part 1: A film or sound piece**

Submit one coherent piece of no more than 3 minutes. The university would rather see a complete scene or a coherent excerpt than a simple montage of highlights (a showreel).

**Part 2: Contextual Statement**

No more than 300 words, explaining:

- What you did on the piece
- Which results are most worth showing
- Which films you like, and why
- Which area interests you, such as cinematography, sound, or production design
- Why you are suited to this course

**Part 3: Process material**

Up to 8 pages, which can show storyboards, shooting plans, sketches, technical designs, exploratory work, and so on.

### 5. Career paths after graduation

- Directing or screenwriting for fiction film
- Cinematography and lighting
- Film editing and sound
- Producing and production design
- Independent filmmaking
- Film festivals, distribution, and exhibition
- Further study in a filmmaking-related master's degree

It is important to distinguish that roles such as director usually require a long track record of work, collaborative experience, and an industry network; they are not positions one can step into directly upon earning this degree.

**Good fit for:** Students with a long-term creative interest in film who want to make fiction or documentary films, are willing to learn different screen roles, and want to gradually build a personal portfolio.

## 7. How to Choose Between the Three Media and Film Programmes

The programmes most easily confused are UCL Media, UAL Film and Television, and UAL Film Practice.

| Dimension | UCL Media | UAL Film and Television | UAL Film Practice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main focus | Digital media and emerging media | Film, television, and programme production | Filmmaking and production |
| Theory | Important | Integrated with practice | Integrated with practice |
| Hands-on production | Yes | Extensive | Extensive |
| Short films | Yes | Yes | One of the core elements |
| TV studio | Not a main strand of the course | Has dedicated specialist modules | Not a main emphasis |
| Games and interaction | Important content | Not a main focus | Not a main focus |
| AI, VR, AR | Has clearly related modules | Not a main focus | Not a main focus |
| Final output | Research and creative projects | Screen portfolio and research | Graduation film and research |
| Main distinguishing feature | Interdisciplinary | Multiple screen-industry workflows | Cinematic storytelling and professional craft |

The three programmes correspond to three different leanings:

- Want to study the relationship between digital technology and media while doing creative production: UCL Media
- Want to take part in broad screen production across film, television, documentary, and more: UAL Film and Television
- Want to focus on the craft of filmmaking, fiction film, and film creation: UAL Film Practice

There is no simple, absolute ranking among the three; what matters is how well the course fits the student's goals.

## 8. Key Analysis: What Does It Mean for One Person to Apply to These Five Programmes?

This is the most critical question in the whole set of applications.

### 1. This combination spans clearly different disciplines

Looking at course content, the connections among the five programmes are roughly as follows:

- The two mathematics programmes have highly similar bodies of knowledge.
- The two UAL programmes have highly similar bodies of knowledge.
- UCL Media has a clear overlap with the two UAL programmes in hands-on film work.
- There is no comparably direct overlap in coursework between mathematics and film.

So this application can be understood as having two main strands running in parallel: mathematics and creative media.

That said, the five choices alone are not enough to determine which direction the student prefers, nor to infer their motivation for applying.

### 2. Can these five programmes form a coherent personal academic story?

Yes, but it has to be backed by real experience, not just a personal statement that forces the five programmes together.

One possible academic interest is:

Mathematics + Computational Creativity + Digital Media + Film

For example:

- The student has a deep interest in geometry, probability, or computational mathematics;
- They have also used programming, algorithms, and imaging technology to complete creative projects;
- They have explored the relationship between mathematical models and computer graphics or visual presentation;
- And they have actually made films, building experience in scripting, storyboarding, cinematography, or editing.

With this kind of background, mathematics and media can be linked through computational creativity, digital imaging, and visual technology.

To give a concrete example:

Suppose the student has made a short film using programmatically generated visual effects.

The student could then examine this project from different disciplinary angles:

| Perspective | Questions to explore |
| --- | --- |
| Mathematics | Geometric transformations, mathematical models, the principles behind the algorithms |
| UCL Media | How digital visuals affect storytelling and the audience experience |
| Film and Television | How to integrate visual effects into a screen production workflow |
| Film Practice | How to express a creative theme through visual language, editing, and sound |

This would be an application story with a genuine interdisciplinary foundation.

But it must be stressed: the above is only a possible case, not a judgment about this student's actual experience.

### 3. The biggest problem: there is only one UCAS Personal Statement

This is the main risk in this set of applications.

For UK undergraduate entry through UCAS, applicants can generally choose up to five programmes, and these five use up all five choices.

The applicant has only one UCAS Personal Statement, which has to serve all of these programmes at once.

Furthermore:

- Imperial does not accept a separate, alternative personal statement.
- UCL generally does not accept a revised or additional personal statement either, unless the university asks for one.
- UCL allows applications to two different programmes at the same time, but each one takes up a separate UCAS choice.

For 2027 entry, the statement uses a three-question structure:

| UCAS question | Writing goal |
| --- | --- |
| Why do you want to study this course or subject? | Explain why you want to study the subject |
| How have your qualifications and studies helped you to prepare? | Show how your academic studies so far have prepared you |
| What else have you done outside education, and why is it useful? | Connect experiences outside the classroom to your preparation for the subject |

The full text must not exceed 4,000 characters (including spaces), with at least 350 characters per question.

#### Why does this create difficulty?

Suppose the applicant writes a statement focused mainly on mathematics:

> What interests me most is mathematical proof, abstract algebra, and number theory, and I hope to explore the logical structure of mathematics further.

This is clearly relevant to the mathematics programmes, but for UAL Film Practice it may not adequately explain why the student wants to train in filmmaking.

Conversely, if the statement mainly says:

> I want to become a film director, and I love expressing emotion through the language of the camera and visual storytelling.

it helps show an interest in film, but falls short of demonstrating the academic motivation in mathematics that an Imperial Mathematics application requires.

Moreover, this tension cannot be resolved simply by adding a few sentences to the statement saying "I also love math and film."

It is especially important to note: universities usually do not see all of an applicant's choices at other institutions when making admissions decisions, but they do see the same personal statement. So the issue is not that the combination of five choices will automatically be judged unreasonable, but whether the case each programme sees is targeted enough.

## 9. How to Judge Whether This Application Makes Sense

Below are several different real-world scenarios.

### Scenario A: The student is very strong in mathematics and also has real film work

If the student has an excellent academic background in mathematics and has already completed short films, documentaries, or interactive media work, this combination has a reasonable basis.

But the applicant still needs to prepare two separate sets of evidence:

**Mathematics:**

- Mathematical theory and proof-writing ability
- Mathematical exploration beyond classroom requirements
- Mathematical reading, research, or competition experience
- Preparation for admissions tests such as TMUA

**Media and film:**

- Film or new media work
- Creative process, research, and reflection
- Specific personal contributions to the work
- Exploration of visual storytelling or media technology

For this kind of student, the core challenge is not necessarily ability, but how to give both interests adequate proof within limited application text.

### Scenario B: The student has very good math grades, but their real interest is film

In this case, it is worth asking:

Why still choose mathematics at Imperial and UCL?

If mathematics is chosen only because of good grades or as an "insurance" option for other programmes, it is important to understand that both universities' mathematics courses are highly theoretical, and meeting the grade requirements does not guarantee the course will suit the student.

Once at university, analysis, algebra, differential equations, and mathematical proof will make up the bulk of study.

This is a completely different day-to-day learning experience from sustained filmmaking.

### Scenario C: The student wants to study mathematics, and film is just a hobby

Here, whether the three media and film choices are suitable is also worth reconsidering.

That is because UAL Film Practice and Film and Television are fairly specialized, career-oriented practice courses. Students need to do a large amount of production work and take an active part in team projects.

If the student does not want to work in this creative field long term, using these programmes as safety choices for a pure mathematics application may not be an ideal strategy.

### Scenario D: The student is serious about both mathematics and creative technology

In that case, UCL Media may indeed be a direction with a cross-disciplinary character.

But it is important to understand:

UCL Media is not a combined version of mathematics, computer science, AI, and film.

It centers on media theory and creative media practice. Although students can take modules in creative AI, VR/AR, and so on, its core training direction is still not advanced mathematics.

If the student clearly wants to do computer graphics research, visual algorithms, or machine learning engineering in the future, they still need to compare computer science and related mathematics and engineering courses.

## 12. Overall Assessment of This Set of Applications

This combination of five programmes has three points that deserve particular attention.

### First: Mathematics and film are two clearly different undergraduate training paths

A mathematics degree mainly studies abstract theory, rigorous proof, and mathematical models.

A film degree focuses on developing creative expression, filmmaking craft, storytelling ability, and teamwork.

Although these abilities can support each other in the digital creative field, they are not the same kind of disciplinary training.

So choosing a programme in the end essentially means deciding which kind of training to devote the next three years to.

### Second: UCL Media is an interdisciplinary option, but it cannot fully replace either side

UCL Media can connect digital culture, technology, interaction design, and moving-image creation.

For creators with technical interests, it may be fairly attractive.

But if the student's goal is advanced mathematical research, it cannot replace a mathematics BSc; and if the goal is complete professional training in filmmaking craft, it also needs to be compared carefully against the details of the UAL courses.

### Third: What really needs solving is the coherence of the application narrative

If these five programmes are already decided, my recommendation is to start preparing the application materials by drawing up a clear table mapping experiences to programmes:

| Type of actual experience | Programmes it can support |
| --- | --- |
| In-depth study of mathematical theorems, proofs, and mathematical models | Imperial Mathematics, UCL Mathematics |
| Visual creation using programming or algorithms | UCL Media, and possibly an applied interest in mathematics |
| Making short films with a complete narrative | UCL Media, both UAL film programmes |
| Taking part in team film production in a specific role | Both UAL film programmes |
| Researching digital media, interactive technology, and their social impact | UCL Media |
| An independent project combining mathematical problems with computational imaging | Could serve as supporting evidence for both mathematics and media, but must be explained according to each course's focus |

The point of this table is to prove interest in a subject starting from what the student has actually done, rather than first inventing a story that sounds interdisciplinary and then looking for experiences to fit it.

## Final Conclusion

Looking purely at how the programmes relate to each other:

- **Imperial Mathematics and UCL Mathematics**: one complete mathematics application track.
- **UCL Media, UAL Film and Television, UAL Film Practice**: one creative track running from digital media to professional filmmaking.
- **The most obvious application challenge**: not that the five programmes cannot be applied to together, but that the disciplines are far apart, and a single UCAS personal statement has to respond to completely different academic expectations at once.

If the student has solid, genuine experience in both mathematics and film, this can be a reasonably justified cross-disciplinary set of applications; if it is mainly a way to combine the reputations and entry requirements of different universities, the fit with each programme and the learning experience after enrollment need to be assessed carefully.

What I most recommend verifying further are three pieces of information about this student: their specific A-Level/IB subjects and predicted grades, whether they already have a film or media portfolio, and whether they would rather pursue mathematical research, digital creative work, or filmmaking. These three points say more about whether these five choices suit this student than comparing university rankings alone.
