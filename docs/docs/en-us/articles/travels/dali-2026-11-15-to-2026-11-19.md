## November 16 · Erhai’s east shore {#nov-16}

**Route:** Homestay → Shuanglang Ancient Town → Wase Pier → Xiaoputuo → Wenbi Village → Xiangyang Village → homestay.

Travel north to south along Erhai’s east shore. Use a car between stops. Keeping the same car for the day reduces time spent finding rides, though you can also hail separate rides for each leg.

| Time | Place | Plan | Transport |
|---|---|---|---|
| 08:30–09:30 | Homestay | Breakfast | — |
| 09:30–10:40 | To Shuanglang | Leave Xiangyang Village for the public parking area in Shuanglang Ancient Town | Car, about 45–70 minutes |
| 10:40–12:15 | Shuanglang Ancient Town | Walk from the town’s lanes toward Yuji Bridge, then explore the lakeside path, shops, and Bai residential quarter | Walk |
| 12:15–13:20 | Shuanglang | Lunch and a break | Walk |
| 13:20–14:15 | Wase Pier | Follow East Erhai Ring Road to Wase and visit the public area around the pier | Car, about 25–40 minutes |
| 14:15–15:25 | Haiyin Village, Xiaoputuo | Go to Haiyin Village across from Xiaoputuo and visit the lakeshore around the island | Car, about 10–20 minutes, then walk |
| 15:25–18:30 | Wenbi Village | Follow East Erhai Ring Road to Wenbi Village; explore its lanes and lakeside road, and stay until dusk | Car, about 15–30 minutes, then walk |
| 18:30–20:00 | Xiangyang Village, homestay | Return to Xiangyang Village, have dinner, and go back to the homestay | Car, about 10–25 minutes |

### Stops and food along the way

**Shuanglang Ancient Town:** Walk in from the public parking area and explore Yuji Bridge and the lakeside lanes. Public areas of the old town are free. For lunch, try **Shuanglang Cultural Courtyard Lakeview Restaurant (双廊文化大院海景餐厅)** near Yuji Bridge. It serves Bai-style hot-and-sour fish, vegetables, and Yunnan home cooking.

**Wase Pier:** Visit the public area around the pier for about 30–45 minutes, then continue along the shore to Haiyin Village.

**Xiaoputuo:** A small island in the lake near Haiyin Village. This itinerary visits the public walking area on shore; no boat is needed.

**Wenbi Village:** The village has public viewpoints, cafés, and places to rest. Have dinner here in the evening or return to Xiangyang Village for dinner.

**Dinner:** In Xiangyang Village, try the copper-pot beef hot pot at **Minjiayuan (民家苑)**, No. 1, Group 6, Xiangyang Village, or have Bai home cooking near the homestay.

### Transport and admission

- Public areas of Shuanglang Ancient Town, Wase Pier, the shore across from Xiaoputuo, and Wenbi Village are planned as **free to visit**.
- Some sections of G348 on East Erhai Ring Road are under road maintenance, and traffic may be released in alternating directions. If there is a long delay, shorten the Wase Pier stop while keeping Shuanglang, Xiaoputuo, and Wenbi Village in the itinerary.
- Drive times are planning estimates; follow live navigation on the day.

### Sights and meals

**Dali Ancient Town:** Enter through the South Gate and walk along Fuxing Road, past Wuhua Tower, and along Renmin Road. The main streets and public lanes are free. The old town has Bai architecture, handicraft shops, local snacks, and restaurants.

**Longkan Pier:** On the edge of the Erhai Ecological Corridor on the lake’s west shore. Walk out and back on the public lakeside paths; adjust the distance to suit your schedule. Motor vehicles are restricted inside the corridor, so arrange the ride-hailing drop-off on the perimeter road.

**Lunch and dinner:** In the old town, try cold chicken rice noodles, ersi rice noodles, rushan milk curd, or steam-pot chicken. **Zai Huishou Chicken Rice Noodles (再回首鸡肉米线, Renmin Road Branch 1)** on Renmin Road is an option for lunch or dinner; nearby Yunnan restaurants are another choice.

### Transport and admission

- Public streets in Dali Ancient Town and the public walking areas around Longkan Pier and the Erhai Ecological Corridor are free.
- Dali Ancient Town and Longkan Pier are relatively close; a ride-hailing car is convenient between them. Follow on-site rules for pedestrian areas and the ecological corridor.
- If traffic on the west shore is slow, shorten the afternoon stop at Longkan Pier and keep the time to return to the old town in the evening.

## November 18 · Cangshan Mountains {#nov-18}

**Route:** Homestay → Gantong Ropeway up → Qingbi Stream → Yudai Cloud Roaming Road northbound → Qilongnü Pool → Zhonghe Temple → Zhonghe Ropeway down → homestay. Plan for about **7 hours on the mountain**, roughly **11.5 km on foot**, and two one-way ropeway rides.

This is a south-to-north crossing from Gantong Ropeway to Zhonghe Ropeway. The 11.5 km figure is an estimate in a public travel guide for the route between the two ropeways. Dali Travel describes the full Yudai Cloud Roaming Road as about 18 km; the figures use different start/end points and treatment of side paths, so the distance on site may differ. Stay on the open scenic-area trail; do not follow a map-generated route across the slopes.

These are target times assuming smooth connections, not guaranteed ropeway departures. The two hiking sections total about 11.5 km, with roughly 3 hours of planned moving time, plus about 10 minutes at Qilongnü Pool. This is a brisk pace: keep photography stops short on the early sections and do not run to make up time.

### Vegetarian meal and timing risk

A public travel guide reports that Zhonghe Temple’s vegetarian meal starts at about **11:30**, costs **¥20 per person**, and is available only while supplies last. This is not a real-time promise from the scenic area. The itinerary reaches the temple around **12:40**, so the meal may already be sold out or no longer being served. Do not count on it being available. Ask ropeway staff or the temple about that day’s service before setting out, and carry lunch and high-energy snacks. If the meal is the day’s top priority, adjust your hiking pace or route as on-site staff advise.

### Ropeways, tickets, and access checks

This itinerary requires a **one-way uphill ticket for Gantong Ropeway and a separate one-way downhill ticket for Zhonghe Ropeway**; it is not a round trip on one ropeway. Available public information does not establish whether both one-way tickets can be bought on November 18, whether a combined ticket can be used across ropeways, or the final total including scenic-area admission. No tickets have been purchased. Before buying at the Gantong Ropeway lower station, confirm each of the following:

- Whether one-way uphill Gantong and one-way downhill Zhonghe tickets can be purchased separately, and whether each ticket must be used at its corresponding ropeway station.
- Whether the entire trail from Qingbi Stream to Zhonghe Temple is open, with no temporary closure or visitor limit.
- Whether Zhonghe Ropeway will run downhill as planned and the latest time to board.

The Dali Tourist Distribution Center’s published timetable, dated **March 15, 2017**, lists Gantong Ropeway service in both directions from **09:00 to 15:00**, then downhill only from **15:00 to 17:00**; for Zhonghe Ropeway it lists both directions from **09:00 to 13:00**, then downhill only from **13:00 to 17:00**. This old timetable is for reference only. Follow the ropeway stations’ notices for the day’s hours, suspensions, and ticket sales. If you cannot confirm the Zhonghe downhill ticket and trail access, do not start the one-way crossing. Take a safe route confirmed by staff and descend using a ropeway they confirm is operating.

### Photography and safety

**09:20–09:30, Qingbi Stream:** Photograph the stream, rock walls, and light in the woodland at the open viewpoints before starting along Yudai Road. **09:30–12:40, Yudai Road:** Focus on woodland, gorges, and distant views of Erhai; keep moving to schedule. **13:10–15:30, around Zhonghe Temple:** Photograph the temple, layers of woodland, and distant views of Erhai and Dali Old Town; use a wide lens for the setting and a telephoto lens to compress the ridges and townscape. **16:00–16:30, descending on Zhonghe Ropeway:** From the open chairlift, try photographing the forest, Erhai, and the Dali plain; the view and light depend on weather and seat direction.

Stay seated on the chairlift. Do not lean out, stand, or extend a camera or phone beyond the chair; attach wrist straps to phones and cameras. The Cangshan trail is around **2,600 m** above sea level. The mountainside is usually colder than the lakeshore. Bring a warm layer, a windproof outer layer, gloves, grippy hiking shoes, about **1.5–2 L of water**, and a backup lunch. In strong wind, rain, ice, or if staff instruct you to stop, follow their restrictions and do not force the crossing to catch a ropeway. If you miss a time checkpoint, ask staff immediately for the nearest open descent route.

### Weather reference for the day

China Weather published a forecast for **Dali City** at **11:30 on November 17, 2026**, with temperatures around **5–18°C**. The Yunnan channel of People’s Daily reported snowfall on Cangshan on **November 9**, followed by clouds and mist over the summit and mid-mountain slopes. This does not establish that the trail will still be icy on November 18, nor does it confirm that the entire route will be dry. A city forecast cannot represent conditions at around 2,600 m on the mountainside. Bring warm and windproof layers and gloves, and check mountain conditions, trail access, and ropeway operations again before buying tickets in the morning. If you find ice, strong wind, or trail restrictions, do not start or continue the crossing.

### Luoquan Peninsula and lunch

**Luoquan Peninsula:** On Erhai’s east shore. The scenic area includes Tianjing Pavilion, Luoquan Tower, and lakeside paths. Start at Tianjing Pavilion and walk toward Luoquan Tower. The publicly listed reference price for a standard ticket starts at **¥28/person**; climbing the tower may cost extra. Confirm the day’s prices at the scenic area ticket window or through an official channel.

**Lunch in Xiangyang Village:** Have copper-pot beef at Minjiayuan or choose local home cooking, ersi rice noodles, or similar dishes near the homestay. Keep the afternoon within Xiangyang Village on Erhai’s east shore so it is easy to return to the homestay for luggage.
